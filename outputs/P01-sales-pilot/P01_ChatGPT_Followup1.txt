# Maple & Main: Explaining September's Net-Sales Increase

Comparison period: August 2026 versus September 2026\
Dataset: `sales_validation_v0.csv`\
Currency: INR (₹), excluding tax and shipping

September net sales increased by ₹5,550.00 compared with August, from ₹27,700.00 to ₹33,250.00.

The channel-level analysis shows that Online accounted for almost all of the increase, while Store sales increased only slightly.

## 2. Absolute change and contribution to total increase

### Calculation method

For each channel:

\\[ \text{Absolute Change}=\text{September Sales}-\text{August Sales} \\]

The percentage contribution to the total increase is:

\\[ \text{Contribution}= \frac{\text{Channel's Absolute Change}} {\text{Total Net-Sales Increase}}\times100 \\]

The total increase is:

\\[ 33,250-27,700=\text{₹5,550.00} \\]

### Results

| Channel | August     | September  | Absolute change | Contribution to increase |
| ------- | ---------- | ---------- | --------------- | ------------------------ |
| Online  | ₹12,120.00 | ₹17,550.00 | +₹5,430.00      | 97.84%                   |
| Store   | ₹15,580.00 | ₹15,700.00 | +₹120.00        | 2.16%                    |
| Total   | ₹27,700.00 | ₹33,250.00 | +₹5,550.00      | 100.00%                  |

### Online contribution

\\[ \frac{17,550-12,120}{5,550}\times100 \\]

\\[ =\frac{5,430}{5,550}\times100 =\boxed{97.84\\%} \\]

### Store contribution

\\[ \frac{15,700-15,580}{5,550}\times100 \\]

\\[ =\frac{120}{5,550}\times100 =\boxed{2.16\\%} \\]

Interpretation: Online contributed ₹5,430.00 of the ₹5,550.00 total increase. Store contributed the remaining ₹120.00.

These percentages describe each channel's contribution to the absolute net-sales increase. They are not the channels' own growth rates.

For additional context, the channel-specific growth rates were:

The comparison period for all three growth rates is August 2026 to September 2026.

## 3. Can we investigate units sold, returns, discounts, and product mix?

Yes. The supplied dataset contains the necessary fields for numerical analysis of these factors.

However, numerical comparisons identify what changed in the recorded sales. They do not independently establish why those changes occurred.

### A. Units sold and returned

The dataset records `Units_Sold` and `Units_Returned` for each product line.

| Metric         | August 2026 | September 2026 | Change |
| -------------- | ----------- | -------------- | ------ |
| Units sold     | 76          | 90             | +14    |
| Units returned | 6           | 6              | 0      |
| Units retained | 70          | 84             | +14    |

Units retained are calculated as:

\\[ \text{Units Retained}=\text{Units Sold}-\text{Units Returned} \\]

For August:

\\[ 76-6=70 \\]

For September:

\\[ 90-6=84 \\]

Observed: September recorded 14 more units sold than August, while the number of returned units remained at 6.

This supports a numerical comparison of sales volume and returns. It does not establish whether the increase came from more customers, larger orders, increased store traffic, or other factors.

### B. Discounts

The dataset records `Discount_Rate` for every product line.

Discounts can be analyzed by:

- Comparing the recorded discount rates between August and September.
- Calculating the total discount amount associated with each month's sales.
- Comparing discount rates by channel and product.
- Evaluating how discounts affect net sales.

For example, the unit-weighted average discount rate is:

\\[ \text{Weighted Discount Rate}= \frac{\sum(\text{Units Sold}\times\text{Discount Rate})} {\sum\text{Units Sold}} \\]

Using the supplied data:

| Metric                      | August 2026 | September 2026 |
| --------------------------- | ----------- | -------------- |
| Unit-weighted discount rate | 7.89%       | 9.67%          |

The unit-weighted discount rate increased by approximately 1.77 percentage points from August to September.

Observed: The recorded discount rates, weighted by units sold, were higher in September.

This does not establish that discounts caused the increase. Higher discounts may have increased demand, but the dataset does not provide a counterfactual showing what sales would have been without those discounts.

The dataset supports descriptive analysis and numerical decomposition of net sales.

It does not contain customer-level data, marketing campaign information, competitor data, or a control group. Therefore, it cannot establish external causes.

## 4. Can we conclude that marketing caused the increase?

No. The supplied dataset does not establish that marketing caused September's net-sales increase.

The data establishes the following:

- September net sales increased by ₹5,550.00 compared with August.
- Online contributed 97.84% of this increase.
- Units sold increased from 76 in August to 90 in September.
- The unit-weighted discount rate increased from 7.89% in August to 9.67% in September.
- Storage Basket recorded the largest product-level net-sales increase.

These observations are consistent with several possible explanations, including successful marketing, higher demand, increased discounts, seasonal effects, or changes in customer purchasing behavior.

However, the dataset does not record marketing expenditure, campaign dates, impressions, clicks, conversions, customer acquisition, or a control group.

Consequently, marketing success remains a possible explanation rather than an established cause.

To investigate marketing causality, Maple & Main would need campaign-level data and a suitable comparison, such as a controlled experiment or a credible comparison of customers or locations exposed and not exposed to marketing.
